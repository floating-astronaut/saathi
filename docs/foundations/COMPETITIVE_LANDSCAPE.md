# Competitive landscape

Structural facts (price, payer, funding, contracts) are more reliable in this
space than outcome claims. Every company below states or implies some benefit
to the elder's wellbeing; almost none of that is independently verified. Read
the "honest gap" line for each as the actual competitive analysis — the
product descriptions are just context.

---

## Indian eldercare (human-service model, not AI)

### Emoha
In-home elder-care membership, Gurugram, founded 2018. Tiers: Empower
Advanced ₹2,999/mo, Premium ₹7,999/mo, Elite ₹15,999/mo, plus specialist
add-ons (₹599–₹14,299/mo) for diabetes, dementia, cancer care.¹ Includes
24/7 emergency coordination, ambulance dispatch, doctor-on-call, nurse
check-ins. **Payer: adult children**, marketed explicitly as "gift care to
your parents." No independent outcome evidence (hospitalization reduction,
etc.) found — claims on the site are descriptive, not study-backed.

**Honest gap:** this is paid human-staffed care infrastructure requiring
active subscription and coordination — a different, much more expensive
category than a free-standing conversational companion. It solves "my parent
needs a nurse to call," not "my parent needs help remembering their pills and
navigating a confusing app." Complementary, not really competing.

### Samarth (elder care)
Samarth Life Management, founded 2016. Claims 30,000+ members across 110+
cities (marketing pages elsewhere on the same site say "5,000+ families,"
which don't reconcile with each other — treat both as unaudited self-reported
figures). Self-describes as "India's No.1 Elder Care Organisation," uncited.
Plans: Comprehensive Care Gold ₹10,999/mo, Platinum ₹16,999/mo, Companion
Care ₹12,999/mo, priced per family.² **Payer:** adult children, frequently
NRIs coordinating remote care. Uses WhatsApp/phone as a contact channel to a
human care team — not an AI product.

**Honest gap:** same category as Emoha — human-coordinated case management,
not a daily-use companion. WhatsApp here is a channel to reach staff, not the
product itself, which is the entire distinction Saathi is betting on.

### KITES Senior Care
Bengaluru-founded (2016) clinical/geriatric care specialist — 340+ beds
across 5 centers (Bengaluru, Hyderabad, Chennai) plus home care. Pricing:
part-time home care ₹15,000–25,000/mo; 24/7 dementia home care
₹40,000–60,000/mo; facility packages from ~₹52,000/mo.³ **Payer:** family,
out-of-pocket — no insurance integration found.

**Honest gap:** entirely different category — clinical/nursing labor,
high-cost, institutional. Useful as a reference point for "what a real
medical crisis costs" but not a direct competitor.

### Primus Senior Living
Bengaluru startup, founded 2024, raised **$20M seed** (General Catalyst,
Nikhil Kamath, Gruhas — Oct 2024) to build a "full-stack senior care
platform" spanning healthcare, wellness, lifestyle, intergenerational
living.⁴ Pre-product-launch as of this research — no public pricing, live
product, or user numbers found. Coverage is entirely funding-announcement
driven.

**Honest gap:** well-capitalized and worth monitoring, but unproven and
aimed at a "senior living" (physical/community) category, not a WhatsApp
companion. Not a live competitor yet.

## WhatsApp-based AI assistants

### SeniorTalk
US-based AI chatbot delivering companionship over WhatsApp, Facebook
Messenger, SMS, and phone calls. $10/mo (chat via WhatsApp/Messenger/SMS) or
$20/mo (adds calls).⁵ Markets scam-detection and passive cognitive-decline
monitoring via language-pattern analysis — a product claim, not a cited
clinical validation.

**Honest gap:** English/US-phone-number only today (international "on the
roadmap"), no Indic-language support, no India-specific integrations
(document/photo reading tuned to Indian ID/prescription formats, local
shopping). This is the closest structural analog to Saathi anywhere found,
and it does not currently serve this market at all.

### Elder AI Companion (GS Labs)
India-based, daily check-in *calls* with a caregiver summary. Voice-call-based,
not WhatsApp-native.⁶ Minimal independent coverage beyond the vendor's own
site — treat all claims as unverified marketing.

**Honest gap:** phone-call delivery, not WhatsApp — misses the "distribution
is already solved" bet that anchors Saathi's whole approach.

## Global AI/tech companions for isolated seniors

### ElliQ (Intuition Robotics)
Physical tabletop device (not a phone app), voice-driven, reminders and
wellness check-ins. ~$600/year + $250 installation retail, but significantly:
**offered fully subsidized through state aging agencies, non-profits, and
Medicaid Managed Care Organizations** — Washington State created the first
statewide Medicaid reimbursement code for it (2024).⁷ **Payer:** state/
Medicaid programs and health plans, not typically the elder.

Evidence is the most substantial in this list, but still short of rigorous:
a company/state-agency report (n=107, 62% survey response rate) cites a
**"95% reduction in loneliness"** — real engagement/survey data, not an RCT.
Academic reviewers cited alongside this explicitly call for larger randomized
trials.⁸

**Honest gap:** requires buying, shipping, and installing a dedicated
device — real cost and adoption friction versus an app on a phone the elder
already has. English-language, entirely dependent on US institutional
funding models with no India equivalent.

### Papa (Papa Inc.)
Human companionship-on-demand — "Papa Pals" visit for errands, housework,
company. Not AI. ~100 US Medicare Advantage plans include it as a benefit
(often 36 free hours/year); out-of-pocket $25–30/hr.⁹ **Payer:** US health
insurers, via capitation/extra-benefit credit, rarely the elder directly.
KFF Health News coverage is explicitly skeptical — plans get benefit-richness
credit for offering it, with limited public data on actual utilization or
outcomes.¹⁰

**Honest gap:** gig-economy human labor, US-insurance-dependent, no India
equivalent, and deliberately not software-scalable.

### Care.coach
Tablet + always-on human-monitored avatar for reminders, conversation, and
family video calls. $199/mo, HIPAA-compliant.¹¹ **Payer:** family or
facility, marketed direct and to senior-care organizations.

**Honest gap:** dedicated hardware again, English-only, US pricing far above
what Indian households would pay for this category, and staffed by live
humans behind the avatar — not the pure-AI, marginal-cost-near-zero shape
Saathi is aiming for.

### GrandPad
Locked-down tablet for seniors ($299–599 device + $25–95/mo). Some US
Medicaid managed-care plans (e.g. Minnesota's MSHO) provide it free to
eligible members.¹² 

**Honest gap:** dedicated device, locked ecosystem — not reachable through
an app the elder already has installed, which is the whole thesis Saathi is
testing against.

## Skepticism, stated plainly

Across this list, the things that hold up under scrutiny are **structural**:
actual price points, actual insurer/Medicaid contracts, actual funding
rounds. The things that don't hold up as well are **outcome claims**:
Emoha's and Samarth's implicit "we prevent crises" framing has no cited
study behind it at all; ElliQ's 95%-loneliness-reduction figure is real
engagement data from a small, non-randomized sample, explicitly flagged by
its own cited academic reviewers as needing a proper trial; Samarth's two
member-count figures on its own site don't agree with each other.

**The gap Saathi is actually betting on**, stated plainly against this
landscape: every AI-companion competitor found requires either a dedicated
device (ElliQ, Care.coach, GrandPad — real cost and shipping/setup friction)
or a market Saathi doesn't operate in (US Medicaid/insurance-funded,
English-only). Every India-based competitor found is a human-staffed service
sold to adult children as a subscription, not a conversational AI the elder
uses daily on a channel they already have installed. **Nobody found combines
"already-installed WhatsApp," "Indic-language voice-first," and "AI
companion, not human labor"** — which is either a real gap or a sign the
combination hasn't been proven viable yet. This doc can't tell you which;
that's what week 1–8 (PRD §16) is for.

## What could not be verified

- Samarth's actual membership scale — two figures on its own site (30,000+
  members vs. 5,000+ families) don't reconcile; neither is independently
  audited.
- Any independent (non-vendor) outcome study for Emoha, Samarth, or the
  Elder AI Companion (GS Labs) — all claims found are vendor-sourced.
- ElliQ's 95% figure as anything beyond a small-sample engagement survey —
  no RCT located, and reviewers cited alongside the figure say the same.
- Actual live-user numbers or pricing for Primus Senior Living — appears to
  be pre-launch as of this research.
